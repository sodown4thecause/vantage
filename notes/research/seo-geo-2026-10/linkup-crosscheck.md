## llms.txt adoption and evidence of impact on AI search citations 2026

As of 2026, `llms.txt` adoption is real but varies substantially by sample: broad-web studies report roughly **2%–10%**, while technical or self-selected samples report higher rates. For example, the 2025 Web Almanac reported 2.13% of desktop sites, SE Ranking reported 10.13% across about 300,000 domains, and Ahrefs reported 28% in its technically skewed customer sample. These figures are not directly comparable.

**Evidence of impact on AI search citations is weak.** The cited studies report no generalizable citation lift: SE Ranking found that its citation model predicted better without the file as a feature; a matched-pair study reported a difference within measurement noise; and an audit of 94,614 cited URLs found one `llms.txt` URL. Server-log studies also found that most files received no requests, though that does not by itself prove that no system ever uses the format.

The sources describe `llms.txt` as a proposed convention, not a broadly adopted web standard. They report no major AI search provider commitment to use it as a citation or ranking signal. One smaller study reports an approximately 11% lift for Claude and Perplexity, but the supplied evidence does not establish that result as generalizable.

**Bottom line:** Treat `llms.txt` as optional—not a proven AI-citation tactic. Its clearest reported use is helping some developer tools and documentation workflows navigate site content.
