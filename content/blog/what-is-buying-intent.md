**Buying intent** is evidence, in what someone writes, that they are ready to
choose, buy or switch to a solution. In public conversations on Reddit,
Hacker News or X, it is the difference between a person discussing a topic and
a person asking for help deciding.

## What signals show buying intent?

The clearest signals, roughly from strongest to weakest:

- **An explicit ask**: "Can anyone recommend…", "What should I use for…"
- **Named alternatives**: "We're comparing X and Y" or "Looking for an alternative to X"
- **Constraints**: a budget, a team size, a deadline or a required integration
- **A trigger event**: a price increase, an outage, a shutdown announcement
- **Pain described in detail**: someone explaining the workaround they hate

A post with an explicit ask *and* a constraint is about as strong as public
intent gets.

## What looks like intent but is not?

Several kinds of post share the right keywords without the intent:

- **Venting** about a tool, with no wish to change it
- **Jokes and memes** in the topic
- **Vendor posts** promoting their own product
- **Old threads** where the poster has long since decided

## How do you score a conversation?

contextfor.dev uses five scores for each opportunity, because intent alone is not
enough to decide whether a reply is worth writing:

| Score | Question it answers |
|-------|---------------------|
| Fit | How closely does the poster's problem match your product? |
| Intent | Are they asking for a solution or just venting? |
| Evidence | How many posts and sources back the opportunity? |
| Momentum | Is the conversation still growing? |
| Timing | Is it recent enough that a reply will still land? |

A thread can have high intent and poor fit (they want something you do not
build), or perfect fit and poor timing (they decided last month). Ranking on
all five keeps the daily list short.

## What should you do once you find one?

Reply as a person, not a vendor: answer the question, disclose that you build
something relevant, and make the link optional. See
[how to find customers on Reddit without spamming](/blog/find-customers-on-reddit-without-spamming)
for a step-by-step reply structure.
